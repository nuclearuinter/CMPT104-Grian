Looking for some free horror for Halloween? There's plenty to be found online! I've got four of my favourite horror series here to share with you.

Keep in mind the warnings! They're there to warn you about any content that I felt could be upsetting for some (basically, things you wouldn't expect in 'typical' horror).

Newer entries in any of these series may feature content that isn't listed in the content warnings if this page hasn't been updated yet. Additionally, I might just forget to mention something. So, please, don't just take my word for it--do your own research, stay safe, and most importantly...

The Pumpkin Rabbit Series

⚠️ Content warnings! ⚠️

Harm to children, children in distress


There are two full-fledged videos currently featuring the Pumpkin Rabbit, most notable of which is the sequel to The Mysterious House (2020), The Return of the Pumpkin Rabbit (2024). These are disconnected, "non-canon" (Martin, in the description of TMH) short stories from The Walten Files.

It isn't necessary to watch TMH to understand its sequel, TRPR, as it exposits enough information at the start that viewers who haven't seen TMH can understand the setting. However, watching both in chronological order does make you appreciate how much Martin's art has improved!



TMH feels like a cautionary tale or urban legend about Halloween as experienced by the victim, not how you'd tell it around a campfire. There's still a level of detachment from the horror, however, as none of the violence truly happens on screen, only the aftermath. Both are clearly based on old 60s Halloween specials, given the aspect ratio, the intros (especially title cards!), and the grainy effect that makes it seem that it'd been ripped off a tape.

But TRPR is far more cinematic. There's no cut to relieve the audience this time when

the Pumpkin Rabbit catches Ralph. Instead, they are forced to watch as he mutilates Ralph's face, to hear his cold laughter over Ralph's visceral shrieks. Knowing what happened to Ralph, seeing the Pumpkin Rabbit pick up Dyno later on was especially distressing to me, as my younger brother is also semi-nonverbal and autistic, primarily parroting or stimming what he hears.

There's really such an upgrade in quality that the more you watch, the more foreshadowing you'll notice--something there wasn't as much of in TMH. There aren't really any jump scares, something I greatly appreciate because the usage of stingers and sudden pop-ups are cheap methods to "scare" the audience without earning it. And, almost all of the sound is diegetic, meaning that the music you hear is in-universe. It's definitely one of my favorites of all time! (And I love my Dyno, my little guy <3)

Monsters From Planet Monsterous

⚠️ Content warnings! ⚠️

Flashing lights,

Ye VHS Tapes of Olde is a channel that its creator "made to showcase the collection of video cassettes in [their] house". However, the children's series they show, Monsters from Planet Monsterous (1996, according to the tape), doesn't appear to exist. Even stranger, these seemingly official tapes contain... what can only be described as "commercials".

MfPM (or VHS Tapes of Olde, as many shorten it) is a rather obscure series that only began to get some flowers relatively recently. However, it is very much a labor of love, as, to date, this project has taken five years to produce ten videos. There are multiple art mediums involved in each episode. From stop motion, puppetry, 3d and 2d animation, any artist could recognize just how much effort is put into this series. I'd almost call it absurd horror, with how nonsensical, disconnected, and amusing the commercials initially appear. However, I can promise you, there's a reason for all of them.
Monsters from Planet Monstrous (1) (2020)

The horror in this is more of a slow burn, subtly building with each new entry and revelation, as it does with most long-form series like these. However, the more you find, the more questions you'll have, and the harder it is to look away.
It should be noted that this is primarily an Alternate Reality Game, or ARG--a live, fictional scenario that is played as if it is real, wherein users can affect the story, sort of like an escape room! However, that often means it can be a little complex to fully understand these series if you just watch. Fortunately, some videos go over the series so far, including its ARG elements, for those who don't want to research on their own. The Night Archives has an incredibly in-depth two-hour-long video that's perfect to get you up to speed with the story so far--but please, watch the series first! To be honest, I would watch Monsters from Planet Monsterous--the series inside of this series--if it were a real show. It captures the feeling of a late 90s children's television show so precisely. It has the same soft tone as a show like Babar, and ESPECIALLY feels like something akin to the original Bob the Builder. There is just so much passion and dedication put into this fascinating story that it's a shame it receives so little attention. By far, MfPM (10) is its best entry, and I have a feeling it'll only get better from here. The story's not finished yet, so keep on the lookout for MfPM (10)! It may take a while before it comes out, but this is a series that is truly quality over quantity.
