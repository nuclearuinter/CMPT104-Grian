The Pumpkin Rabbit

⚠️ Content warnings! ⚠️

Harm to children, including special needs children; Child death. All of these are on-screen.

"It was just another Halloween night in the lovely town of Nash-Lake when a group of brave young children decided to walk into an abandoned house at the top of the Nash-Lake Hill. What they didn't know is what awaited for them there was far more terrifying than anything they could have expected..." - Unknown narrator (0:32 - 1:03)

There are two full-fledged videos currently featuring the Pumpkin Rabbit, most notable of which is the sequel to The Mysterious House (2020), The Return of the Pumpkin Rabbit (2024). These are disconnected, "non-canon" (Martin, in the description of TMH) short stories from The Walten Files.

It isn't necessary to watch TMH to understand its sequel, TRPR, as it exposits enough information at the start that viewers who haven't seen TMH can understand the setting. However, watching both in chronological order does make you appreciate how much Martin's art has improved!



TMH feels like a cautionary tale or urban legend about Halloween as experienced by the victim, not how you'd tell it around a campfire. There's still a level of detachment from the horror, however, as none of the violence truly happens on screen, only the aftermath. Both are clearly based on old 60s Halloween specials, given the aspect ratio, the intros (especially title cards!), and the grainy effect that makes it seem that it'd been ripped off a tape.

In contrast, TRPR is far more cinematic. There's no cut to relieve the audience this time when:
Spoilers!

the Pumpkin Rabbit catches Ralph. Instead, they are forced to watch as he mutilates Ralph's face, and hear his cold laughter over Ralph's visceral shrieks. Knowing what happened to Ralph, seeing the Pumpkin Rabbit pick up Dyno later on was especially distressing to me. Dyno exhibits many traits of semi-nonverbal autism, where he primarily parrots what he hears or stims by repetition. My younger brother also does this, so it made the scene really heartbreaking and intense!

There's such an upgrade to quality. With each watch, you'll see when events were foreshadowed, or blink-and-you'll-miss-it moments: something there wasn't as much of in TMH. There aren't really any jump scares, something I greatly appreciate because the usage of stingers and sudden pop-ups are cheap methods to "scare" the audience without earning it. Almost all of the sound is diegetic, meaning that the music you hear is in-universe. It's definitely one of my favorites of all time, and I would strongly recommend it if you can stomach horror that involves children!! (And I love Dyno, my little guy <3)

Monsters From Planet Monsterous

⚠️ Content warnings! ⚠️

Flashing lights,

Ye VHS Tapes of Olde is a channel that its creator "made to showcase the collection of video cassettes in [their] house". However, the children's series they show, Monsters from Planet Monsterous (1996, according to the tape), doesn't appear to exist. Even stranger, though these tapes appear to be official, they contain... what can only be described as "commercials".


MfPM (or VHS Tapes of Olde, as many call it) is a rather obscure series that has only begun receiving attention relatively recently. Despite the low , it is very much a labor of love, as, to date, this project has taken five years to produce ten videos. There are multiple art mediums involved in each episode. From stop motion, puppetry, 3d and 2d animation, any artist could recognize just how much effort is put into this series. I'd almost call it absurd horror, with how nonsensical, disconnected, and amusing the commercials initially appear.


Monsters from Planet Monstrous (1) (2020)

The horror in this is more of a slow burn, subtly building with each new entry and revelation, as it does with most long-form series like these. However, the more you find, the more questions you'll have, and the harder it is to look away.

It should be noted that this is primarily an Alternate Reality Game, or ARG--a live, fictional scenario that is played as if it is real, wherein users can affect the story, sort of like an escape room! However, that often means it can be a little complex to fully understand these series if you just watch. Fortunately, some videos go over the series so far, including its ARG elements, for those who don't want to research on their own. The Night Archives has an incredibly in-depth two-hour-long video that's perfect to get you up to speed with the story so far--but please, watch the series first!

To be honest, I would watch Monsters from Planet Monsterous--the series inside of this series--if it were a real show. It captures the feeling of a late 90s children's television show so precisely. It has the same soft tone as a show like Babar, and ESPECIALLY feels like something akin to the original Bob the Builder.

There is just so much passion and dedication put into this fascinating story that it's a shame it receives so little attention. By far, MfPM (10) is its best entry, and I have a feeling it'll only get better from here. The story's not finished yet, so keep on the lookout for MfPM (10)! It may take a while before it comes out, but this is a series that is truly quality over quantity.
