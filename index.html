The Pumpkin Rabbit

⚠️ Content warnings! ⚠️

Harm to children, including special needs children; Child death. All of these are on-screen.

"It was just another Halloween night, in the lovely town of Nash-Lake, when a group of brave young children decided to walk into an abandoned house at the top of the Nash-Lake Hill. What they didn't know is what awaited for them there was far more terrifying than anything they could have expected..."

- Summary by unknown narrator (0:32 - 1:03)
There are two videos currently dedicated to the Pumpkin Rabbit, most notable of which is the sequel to The Mysterious House (2020), The Return of the Pumpkin Rabbit (2024). These are disconnected, "non-canon" (Creator in desc. of TMH) short stories from The Walten Files.

TRPR works as a standalone video, as it exposits enough information at the start that viewers who haven't seen TMH can understand the setting. However, watching both in chronological order allows you to appreciate how much Martin's art has improved, and it also provides a perspective from when Rachel and Lorenzo Waterman were alive!



TMH feels like a cautionary tale or urban legend about Halloween as experienced by the victim, rather than how you'd tell it around a campfire. There's still a level of detachment from the horror, however, as none of the violence truly happens on screen: we only see the aftermath. Both videos are clearly based on old 1960s Halloween specials, given the aspect ratio, the intros (especially the title cards!), and the grainy effect that makes it seem as though they were ripped off a tape.
In contrast, TRPR is far more cinematic. There's no cut to relieve the audience this time when:

the Pumpkin Rabbit catches Ralph. Instead, they are forced to watch as he mutilates Ralph's face, and hear his cold laughter over Ralph's visceral shrieks. Knowing what happened to Ralph, seeing the Pumpkin Rabbit pick up Dyno later on was especially distressing to me. Dyno exhibits many traits of semi-nonverbal autism, where he primarily parrots what he hears or stims by repetition. My younger brother also does this, so it made the scene really heartbreaking and intense!

With each watch, you'll catch new foreshadowing and detail, and this short puts in the work to actually EARN its scares from the audience--there aren't really any jump scares, which increases rewatchability (as it's not just a matter of expecting when they happen)!

As someone who loves little details like this, almost all of the sound is diegetic, meaning that the music you hear is part of the in-universe world, which lends moments of silence a heavy tension. It's definitely one of my all-time favourites (definitely up there with EMESIS BLUE), and I would strongly recommend it if you can stomach horror that involves children!! (also I love Dyno, he's such a little guy <3)

Monsters From Planet Monsterous

⚠️ Content warnings! ⚠️

Flashing lights,

Ye VHS Tapes of Olde is a channel that its creator "made to showcase the collection of video cassettes in [their] house". However, the children's series they show, Monsters from Planet Monsterous (1996, according to the tape), doesn't appear to exist. Even stranger, though these tapes appear to be official, they contain... what can only be described as "commercials".



MfPM (or VHS Tapes of Olde, as many call it) is a rather obscure series that has only begun receiving attention relatively recently. Despite the low , it is very much a labor of love, as, to date, this project has taken five years to produce ten videos. There are multiple art mediums involved in each episode. From stop motion, puppetry, 3d and 2d animation, any artist could recognize just how much effort is put into this series. I'd almost call it absurd horror, with how nonsensical, disconnected, and amusing the commercials initially appear.


Monsters from Planet Monstrous (1) (2020)

The horror in this is more of a slow burn, subtly building with each new entry and revelation, as is often the case with long-form horror series like these. However, the more you find, the more questions you'll have, and the harder it is to look away.

It should be noted that this is primarily an Alternate Reality Game, or ARG--a live, fictional scenario that is played as if it is real, wherein users can affect the story. However, that often means that understanding the series on a surface level can be complex. Fortunately, some videos summarize the series and its ARG elements for those who don't want to research on their own. The Night Archives has an incredibly in-depth two-hour-long video that's perfect to get you up to speed with the story so far--but please, watch the series first!

I would watch Monsters from Planet Monsterous--the series inside of this series--if it were a real show. It captures the feeling of a late 90s children's television show so precisely. It has the same soft tone as a show like Babar, and ESPECIALLY feels like something akin to the original Bob the Builder.

There is just so much passion and dedication put into this fascinating story that it's a shame it receives so little attention. By far, MfPM (10) is its best entry, and I have a feeling it'll only get better from here. The story's not finished yet, so keep on the lookout for MfPM (10)! It may take a while before this series releases a new entry, but it is truly quality over quantity.
